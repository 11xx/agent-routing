# Roster note: a third vendor arrives, and outside evidence lands (2026-08-05 → )

Two things reached the roster within days of each other and belong in one note,
because they exercise the same distinction: what counts as evidence for moving a
seat, and what only counts as knowing something.

Neither moved a routed seat. The head model, the technical peer, the workhorse,
and the reviewer line are all unchanged.

## Muse Spark 1.2 enters as a future row

Meta released Muse Spark 1.2 on 2026-08-05 alongside Muse Code, a terminal
harness. Third-party index scoring places the model level with Grok 4.5 and the
Opus 4.8 band — a band Sol medium dominates — so it enters beside the other
unreachable rows rather than as a tier anything routes to.

Its launch benchmarks are vendor-run, and the reason they cannot be merged into
the pass@1 column is arithmetic rather than suspicion:

| Model | Vendor's DeepSWE v1.1 | This matrix |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| GPT-5.6 Terra · high | 64.8% | 54% |
| Claude Opus 5 | 65.0% | 73% |

Nominally the same benchmark, plainly not the same scale — and the vendor's own
methodology says its tools and prompts may not suit third-party models. Its
predecessor also published a Terminal-Bench figure that verified 3.8 points low,
a gap wider than the stated confidence interval, so the optimism here is
measured rather than assumed. This is the same treatment DeepSeek's self-reported
54.4% received: availability and direction-of-travel evidence, never a row.

Two facts are recorded because they are routing-relevant and will not change
with a better benchmark. The model is hosted-only — no downloadable weights —
and its 21×-cheaper contributor tier is paid for in training rights over the
code sent to it, which is a licensing decision that cannot be taken per task.

Activation order is deliberate: an independent measurement first, then a backend
probe. Probing a closed, login-gated harness is real work, and the capability
case does not currently justify spending it on vendor evidence.

## Outside field evidence, and what it may move

A detailed practitioner account of the same roster landed the same week. Its
value is corroboration from someone with no access to the measurements here, and
it moved four things — none of them a seat.

- **DeepSeek V4 Flash has open weights**, so a posture exists that no hosted
  pool offers: run it locally and no third party sees the prompt. It also has
  no frontier refusal surface, which makes it the practical route for authorized
  security work where hosted models refuse or degrade mid-task. Both are
  recorded as niches. Neither buys trust — a locally hosted fabricator
  fabricates identically, and the generator-only rule stands untouched.
- **Steer that model with exemplars, not prohibitions.** Its raw output is
  functional and graceless; showing it the target is what fixes that, where
  fencing what it may not do yields verbose noise.
- **Grok 4.5 has a first-party harness** (the Grok Build CLI), so its row's
  blocker is an unrun probe rather than an absent mechanism. The capability case
  for running it remains weak.
- **Kimi K3 is independently credited with holding a long autonomous arc**,
  which is precisely the axis pass@1 cannot see. It stays the inactive row most
  worth a probe.

The account also rates Opus 5 far below its position here, and recommends using
it only as a closely supervised, tightly specified subagent. That is a
third-party arrival at this roster's own calibration finding by a different
route, and it argues for keeping the fences rather than vacating the seat.

## The axis the matrix was missing

The most useful import is a frame, not a fact: **task distance is a routing
axis**. A model's error is set at the start, in how it reads the target, and the
longer the arc the further a small misreading compounds. A misinterpretation
that is harmless over a five-line change lands somewhere unrecognisable at the
end of an unattended hour — plausibly complete, green on its gates, and not what
was asked for.

This does not contradict the delegation ladder; it names what the ladder was
already pricing as residual risk after the gate. Two consequences worth stating
outright: cheap tiers are safe over short distances almost regardless of how
well they interpret, and the cost of an underspecified target is paid in
proportion to arc length — so specification effort belongs where the arc is
long, not where the task is hard.
